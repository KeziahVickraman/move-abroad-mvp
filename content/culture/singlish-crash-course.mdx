# Singlish Crash Course: 10 Particles & Phrases to Know

Singlish is Singapore’s beloved colloquial language—an organic fusion of English grammar shortcuts with vocabulary and tone borrowed from Hokkien, Cantonese, Malay, and Tamil.

## 1. The Sentence-Ending Particles
Particles convey subtle nuance, emotional temperature, and social familiarity:

- **Lah (Reassurance or Finality):** "Don't worry lah, the train is coming in two minutes!"
- **Leh (Curiosity, hesitation, or soft reminder):** "Why you didn't tell me earlier leh?"
- **Lor (Resignation or obvious acceptance):** "If it rains then cannot jog outdoors lor."
- **Meh (Skeptical surprise):** "Really meh? I thought the meeting was scheduled for tomorrow?"
- **Sia (Emphasis / Exclamation):** "Wah, this afternoon weather is so humid sia!"

## 2. Essential Vocabulary
- **Shiok (Malay origin):** Expresses pure sensory satisfaction, deliciousness, or comfort ("This iced bandung is so shiok!").
- **Paiseh (Hokkien origin):** Feeling embarrassed, shy, or apologetic ("Paiseh for replying late, was stuck in a call!").
- **Makan (Malay origin):** To eat or have a meal ("Have you makan yet? Let's go Maxwell!").
- **Kiasu (Hokkien origin):** Fear of missing out or losing out on an advantage.
- **Can or cannot?:** A concise, efficient question asking if a request or timeline is feasible ("Can!").
